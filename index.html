Lewis Station Park Restoration

About

About the restoration

Lewis Station is a beloved downtown pocket park at Church Street and Hunt Avenue, about half a block from Main Street. The land was given to the City by Rosa Lee Lewis in 1975, and community members came together to create a seating area with an arbor and trees. Over the years the trees died and the arbor became unusable — and with no significant improvements in 50 years, this ideally located park is calling for a refresh.

Beginning in September 2025, a group of dedicated residents worked through some fourteen resident and City staff meetings — alongside the City's Parks & Recreation Department, landscape designer Becke Oberschulte, and arborist Bill Pramuk — to shape a thoughtful restoration, developed in coordination with the City's Parks and Trails Master Plan. The plan brings healthy shade, drought-tolerant firewise native plantings, restored seating and facilities, and a more welcoming place to gather. It was presented to the Parks & Recreation Commission in June 2026, and the community is now invited to help bring it to life.

Led by Kathy Carrick, Tracy McBride, and Nancy Morrell (Beautification Foundation), with landscape designer Becke Oberschulte and arborist Bill Pramuk.

The Park Today

What's already there

Today the park offers three picnic tables, an accessible bathroom, a plaque honoring Rosa Lee Lewis, an existing Valley Oak, and three other mature trees in its southern corner.

Two railroad artifacts — a Southern Pacific crossing sign and a metal track switch — recall the park's place along the historic Napa Valley rail corridor, and there may also be room for public art.

The Plan

What the restoration includes

Developed by the volunteer committee with City staff — low-water, low-maintenance, fire-smart, and planted to support native bees, butterflies, and birds.

Highlights of the design

  • A central Coast Live Oak — a majestic California native that will one day give 50+ feet of shade.
  • Firewise, drought-tolerant natives — Arbutus, Ceanothus, and Western Redbud, needing minimal water once established.
  • Shade sails over the picnic areas for cool, comfortable gathering — with a garden arbor as a backup option.
  • New seating — three new benches with backs, plus natural boulder seating.
  • Restored facilities — bathroom refresh, fencing along two sides, and a renewed irrigation system and controller.
  • Sheet mulching & soil enhancements plus a new garbage & recycling station to keep the park healthy and tidy.

Maps & the concept plan

The committee's conceptual plan for the park, and where Lewis Station sits in downtown St. Helena.

The park plans

The California native, drought-tolerant plants are chosen to support native bees, butterflies, and birds and once established, may need little to no irrigation. The plantings can be expanded with even more natives over time, serving the community as a native plant demonstration garden.

Conceptual plan

The committee’s proposed planting, seating, and shade design — a central Coast Live Oak, native Arbutus, Ceanothus and Redbud, new benches and boulders, and yellow shade sails over the picnic area.

Progress

Status updates

Follow the restoration from first idea to planting day. We'll keep this timeline current as milestones are reached.

August 2025

Neighbors begin planning

Residents kicked off the effort with roughly ten community meetings, plus four sessions with City staff and a consultation with arborist Bill Pramuk, to review ideas and identify the park's needs — over 50 volunteer hours in all.

June 15, 2026

Presented to the Parks & Recreation Commission

The volunteer group presented the plan to the Commission, with input from Public Works staff.

August 11, 2026

Approved by City Council

The St. Helena City Council authorized accepting donations and pursuing the renovation — with letters of support from residents and community organizations. View the Council meeting record →

Summer 2026 · Underway now

Site prep & fundraising

Public fundraising opens and site preparation begins — removing the invasive Tree of Heaven, transplanting two Catalpa trees, and clearing select pavers.

Fall 2026

Planting, irrigation & community mulching day

City staff install the new plantings and irrigation, neighbors gather for a community sheet-mulching day, and the new benches and boulder seating go in.

Winter 2026 – Spring 2027

Restroom, shade & fencing

Restroom exterior restoration, shade sail installation, fence repair, and possible public art complete the project.

Support

Support the park

Contributions go toward plants, irrigation, mulch, shade, and materials for the restoration. Any amount helps.

Why your support matters

  • A natural park in the center of downtown — for residents, visitors, employees of nearby businesses, children walking home from school, and young adults looking for a place to gather.
  • A welcoming, shaded respite in nature for individuals and groups, with accessible seating and a bathroom for anyone who needs a place to rest or picnic.
  • A point of community pride — an attractive park with real historical significance.
  • Quality infrastructure designed to last, setting the stage for more native planting across our environment.
